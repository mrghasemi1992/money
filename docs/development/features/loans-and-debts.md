# Loans and debts

«طلب و بدهی» / “Loans and debts” (#47) tracks what people owe the book and what the book owes them: money lent to a friend, borrowed from family, a bank loan paid back in installments, or a debt from years before the book that is still being paid off. Each person or lender is an account of type `counterparty`. Money lent, borrowed or repaid moves between it and the book's own accounts as a **transfer**; a debt that no money of the book moved for is a dated **receivable** («طلب») or **payable** («بدهی») on the counterparty. None of these is ever income or expense. Why this model: [decision 17](../decisions.md#17-loans-and-debts-are-transfers-and-dated-debts-on-counterparty-accounts).

[Architecture](../architecture.md) explains how the pieces fit together; [conventions](../conventions.md) the rules for writing code.

## How it works

1. **A person or lender is an account** of type `counterparty` (`ACCOUNT_TYPES`; the book's own kinds are `MONEY_ACCOUNT_TYPES`). Names are unique across all accounts. It has no identifier and no opening balance (always 0, `accounts_counterparty_opening_check`), and is listed on `/debts`, not `/accounts`.
2. **Its balance is what is owed**, from the same `accountBalance()` as every account: positive means they owe the book, negative that the book owes them (`debtStatus`). Lending is a transfer to them (+); borrowing or being repaid a transfer from them (−); repaying them a transfer to them (+); a receivable (+); a payable (−).
3. **A debt no money of the book moved for** (from before the book, or arranged without a payment) is a transaction of type `receivable` («طلب»: they owe the book) or `payable` («بدهی»: the book owes them), with its own date, optional time, description and tags. Several old debts with one person are several dated entries, each in their ledger. This is the "opening balance transaction" of double-entry books (GnuCash's Equity: Opening Balances, Firefly III's opening balance and reconciliation transactions), written as one-sided entries on the counterparty.
4. **Nothing about a loan is income or expense.** Totals, budgets and reports count only `income` and `expense` (`INCOME_EXPENSE_TYPES`), so transfers, receivables and payables never reach them; the unknown tag also applies only to income and expense.
5. **Real costs still count:** when a person pays for the book (Ali paid for dinner), it is an expense from the person's account with the real category, which also makes the book owe them; a forgiven debt is an expense from their account (a gifts category); loan interest is an expense, and the principal of an installment a transfer.

## Details

- **Database rules:** `transactions_type_check` allows `receivable` and `payable`; the generated column `debt_account_type` is `'counterparty'` for them and null otherwise, and the foreign key `transactions_debt_account_fk` `(account_id, debt_account_type)` → `accounts (id, type)` (the target is `accounts_id_type_unique`) makes their account a counterparty and stops that account from changing type while it has them. The transfer check keeps `to_account_id` null for them, and the category foreign key leaves them without a category (no category has their type). A counterparty's opening balance must be 0.
- **Page** (`/debts`, nav item «طلب و بدهی» in the `book` group after accounts, `HandCoinsIcon`): every role reads; viewers get no write controls. `AccountSettings kind="counterparty"` shows the net, what is owed to the book and what it owes (`debtTotals`, summed from the balances SQL computed, like the accounts page's total), a note that loans are transfers, the people and lenders in their order (drag or the row menu), archived ones, and the same add, edit, archive and delete dialogs with the `debts` messages. Each row's quiet line is the status («طلب دارید», «بدهکارید», «تسویه») and the transaction count. The row menu has «ثبت طلب یا بدهی» (writers: opens the shell's add form preset to a receivable on that person, `useAddTransactionWith`) and «مشاهده تراکنش‌ها» (`/transactions?account=<id>&from=<oldest>`, `accountTransactionsHref`, `firstDate` from `accountFirstDate()`; on both pages).
- **Person form** (`AccountDialog kind="counterparty"`): the name only, with a hint that debts are recorded from the row menu and loans as transfers.
- **Transaction form** (`TransactionDialog`): the type control has four kinds (`TRANSACTION_FORM_KINDS`: هزینه، درآمد، انتقال، طلب/بدهی). «طلب/بدهی» keeps every loan action in one place, as people think of them: «چه اتفاقی افتاد؟» (`DEBT_ACTIONS`: «قرض دادم», «قرض گرفتم», «پس گرفتم», «پس دادم»), «طرف حساب» (the counterparties only; a note when there are none) and «از کدام حساب؟» / «به کدام حساب؟» (the book's own accounts, or «هیچ‌کدام، پولی جابه‌جا نشد»). `debtEntry` (`src/helpers/debt.ts`) turns that into what is stored: with an account, a transfer to the person (lent, paid back) or from them (borrowed, paid back to the book); with «هیچ‌کدام», a receivable (lent, paid back) or a payable (borrowed, paid back to the book). So recording a loan from a card always lowers the card's balance, and an old debt never touches one. There is no category or destination account field; the submit button («ثبت قرض» / «ثبت بازپرداخت»), the account hint and the description placeholder follow the action. Editing a receivable or payable opens this kind (as a loan or a borrowing with «هیچ‌کدام»); a transfer opens as a transfer, since lending and paying back are stored alike and the action isn't known. The form's `preset` (type and account) is how the debts page opens it.
- **Lists and detail:** a debt's title is «طلب از علی» / «بدهی به علی» (`transactions.list.receivable` / `payable`), its tile a hand with coins on the transfer colors, its amount neutral (no sign, transfer color) and the row doesn't repeat the account; the detail shows «طرف حساب». The type filter lists «طلب» and «بدهی» with the other types.
- **Elsewhere:** the transaction form's and the filter's account pickers list the book's accounts first and the counterparties after a line, with a person icon (`accountSelectOptions`); the form's default account is never a counterparty. `/accounts`, the dashboard's balances and `list_accounts`' `total_balance` leave counterparties out; the dashboard's first-run steps need an account of the book's own money (`getBookProgress`). Reports' spending by account shows a counterparty when someone paid for the book.
- **Dates years back:** the date picker's calendar title opens the months of the year and then a page of 12 years, and Shift + Page Up/Down moves a year (see [design system](design-system.md)), so a debt from years ago is a few clicks away.
- **Claude and ChatGPT:** `list_accounts` lists counterparties with `type: "counterparty"` and their balance, plus `owed_to_user` and `owed_by_user`. The transaction tools take `receivable` and `payable` (with `account_id` the counterparty; `checkTransactionReferences` refuses another account with `accountNotCounterparty`), and results show them without a category. The instructions say lending, borrowing and repaying are transfers with the counterparty and an old debt a receivable or payable, never «قرض و بدهی» or «طلب» categories; that a payment someone made for the user is an expense from their account; that an installment's interest is an expense; and to ask the user to add a missing person on the page, since the tools can't create accounts.
- **CSV:** the type words include «طلب» / “receivable” / “owed to me” and «بدهی» / “payable” / “I owe”. A receivable or payable row must be on a counterparty: an existing one, or a new name, which the import creates as a counterparty (`NewImportAccount.type`); one of the book's own accounts is the error `debtAccountNotCounterparty`. Their category and destination cells are ignored. The export writes the type in the user's language.
- **Existing «قرض و بدهی» and «طلب» transactions** aren't migrated: the data doesn't say who the other side was. Editing one into a transfer to the right person moves it out of expenses; the categories can then be archived.
- **Not yet:** interest calculation, installment schedules and reminders, splitting one transaction into principal and interest, a currency per person.
